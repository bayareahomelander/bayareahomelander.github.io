Flint GPT-2 from scratch on hand-written CUDA kernels

A CUDA engine that trains GPT-2 (124M) on one consumer GPU and then serves it. Every kernel is written by hand: no cuBLAS, cuDNN, or PyTorch at runtime.

Onyx speeds up decoding on top of existing kernels; this goes one level down, to the kernels themselves. Prior from-scratch projects such as llm.c train GPT-2 without PyTorch, but still hand matrix multiplies and attention to NVIDIA's libraries and target newer GPUs with BF16. The question here is how close hand-written kernels can get to cuBLAS when nothing is borrowed, on a Turing GPU that FlashAttention-2 doesn't support, with the same kernels carried through to inference.

Early result: the matrix multiply already runs at over 80% of cuBLAS on every GPT-2 layer shape. Full benchmarks and logs come with v0.1.

What makes it hard

  • A tensor-core matrix multiply in inline PTX. All three layouts training needs, deterministic split-K, and bias, GELU and residual-add fused into the output stage. Turing lacks the async copies newer GEMMs rely on, and the main loop sits at the 128-register limit.
  • Flash attention, forward and backward. Deterministic with no atomics, inside Turing's 64 KB of shared memory.
  • FP16 training, since Turing has no BF16. Loss scaling throughout; every kernel's error must stay within 2–3× of PyTorch's own FP16 error against an FP32 reference, and runs are bit-identical.
  • Honest measurement. Each kernel is reported as a share of cuBLAS from interleaved runs, and as a share of hardware peak at the clock the GPU actually ran at.

Roadmap

  1. Dec 2026v0.1: the matrix multiply, benchmarked against cuBLAS.
  2. 2027Training: GPT-2 124M end to end on these kernels.
  3. 2027Inference: KV cache, decode attention split across thread blocks, int8 weight-only quantization, and each decode step captured as one CUDA graph. Measured against the memory-bandwidth limit and llama.cpp.
